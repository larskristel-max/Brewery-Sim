# OLD STABLES — Chapter One: **What We Keep**

**Playable story script · 1901–4 August 1914 · Draft canon v1**

## Chapter promise

The Valennes believe they are restoring an old brewhouse to save their château. Over thirteen years, the opposite becomes true: the brewery gives the family income, gives workers leverage, gives the village a common room, and gives Valenne heritage a form that can survive a bad heir. By the time war reaches Belgium, the title can open doors and the château can shelter people, but only the Old Stables can feed, employ and organize them.

This chapter tells one complete rise: a hired brewmaster enters through the servants' gate in 1901 and becomes Estate Steward in 1913. Its final victory is deliberately interrupted. On 4 August 1914 the player opens the same stable doors again—not for a festival, but for mobilized families and people fleeing the eastern roads.

## Canon decisions made for this draft

- **The player character** is addressed as **BREWMASTER** until the player enters a name. The player can customize appearance, first name and training background. For continuity, he was born in 1872; he is twenty-nine in 1901 and forty-two in 1914.
- **Count Armand de Valenne**, fifty-four in 1901, has social access but no governmental authority. His value is introductions, credibility and obligations accumulated over decades—not the power to command officials.
- **Countess Cécile de Valenne**, fifty in 1901, is the family's deliberate network-builder. She remembers births, debts, marriages and favors, and knows that a relationship is only valuable if it has been maintained before it is needed.
- **Apolline de Valenne**, twenty-five in 1901, is their only surviving child and the acting administrator of the estate. She is the player's institutional counterweight and eventual governing partner, never a decorative romantic prize.
- The old Valenne patent transmits the comital title through a male line. To preserve the name, Apolline marries **Laurent de Rivecourt** in 1904. A fictional royal regrant permits their children to bear the Valenne name and makes their eldest son heir to Armand's title. The regrant is won through years of family relationships, not political command.
- Their son **Amaury de Valenne** is born in December 1905. He is the legal heir. Their daughter **Éléonore de Valenne** is born in March 1908. This chapter only plants their opposing dispositions: Amaury absorbs entitlement; Éléonore absorbs responsibility.
- Four inheritances are kept legally and dramatically separate: **the title, the château and land, brewery ownership, and the office of Estate Steward**. Amaury's title will not automatically make him competent, and Éléonore can eventually become the true successor to the Valenne institution even if the title first passes to her brother.
- The original staff remain central: **Jules** (cellar and maintenance), **Maëlle** (packaging and sales), **Noor** (kitchen and hospitality), and **Inez** (stores and purchasing). Their hands and names recur whenever the brewery changes.
- **Compagnie Brassicole des Trois Provinces** is a wholly fictional industrial rival. Its representative, **Victor Sarlet**, offers standardization, distribution and financial safety. He is frequently self-interested and frequently correct.

## Persistent chapter variables

The script refers to four existing player-facing tracks:

- **Runway** — cash and debt pressure.
- **Count's confidence** — Armand's trust in the player's judgment.
- **Community trust** — whether workers, innkeepers and villagers believe the brewery belongs in their future.
- **Restoration** — safe equipment, capacity and the physical recovery of the Old Stables.

Choices change dialogue, available contracts and who comes to the brewery in 1914. They do not break the chapter's spine: the Old Stables reopens, Apolline has two children, the player becomes Steward, and war begins.

# ACT I — FIRST LIGHTS

The exact in-game prologue and awakening dialogue is maintained in [the canonical Act I opening script](opening-script-act-one.md). The sequences below expand its blocking, choices and dramatic consequences.

## Sequence 1 — The Last Horse

**10 January 1901 · Château stable court · Opening cinematic**

**Image:** Blue winter rain. Two bailiff's men lead the last carriage horse through the gate. Their wet gloves are dark against the cream Valenne crest on the animal's old blanket. An empty stall frames the château beyond.

**BLACK.**

Hooves on stone. A chain drawn through an iron ring.

**FADE IN.**

COUNT ARMAND stands in the rain without a hat. COUNTESS CÉCILE watches from under the arch. APOLLINE holds an inked inventory; the paper has gone soft at the edges.

**ARMAND**  
He has left the harness.

**CÉCILE**  
It bears our crest. No buyer wants another family's pride.

One of the bailiff's men avoids Armand's eyes. He touches two fingers to his cap and leads the horse out.

**APOLLINE**  
The treasury carries wages for forty-two days. Less if the north roof fails before spring.

**ARMAND**  
Then we have forty-two days.

**APOLLINE**  
No, Papa. We have debts with dates. That is not the same thing.

Cécile reaches into the empty stall and lifts a large blackened key from a nail.

**CÉCILE**  
The bailiff valued the old brewhouse as rubble.

**APOLLINE**  
Because it has been cold for eleven years.

**ARMAND**  
Stone does not forget its purpose in eleven years.

**APOLLINE**  
Copper forgets nothing. Every year of neglect will be written inside it.

A bicycle bell sounds beyond the gate. The BREWMASTER arrives, soaked, carrying a modest leather tool roll and a letter of training.

Character creation appears over close shots of his hands removing wet gloves: **name, face, coat, training background**.

**CÉCILE**  
Our last applicant has arrived before our last horse has left. That is either providence or very good comic timing.

Player greeting:

1. **“I came to inspect a brewery.”** — practical; Apolline approval.
2. **“I came because the Count offered work.”** — candid; community approval later.
3. **“I came to make beer.”** — confident; Armand approval.

Whatever the response, Apolline offers the wet inventory rather than her hand.

**APOLLINE**  
Forty-two days. No sentimental repairs. No purchase without a cost beside it. If the brewhouse cannot earn more than it consumes, you will tell us plainly.

Armand takes the black key from Cécile and holds it out.

**ARMAND**  
You were engaged as Castle Brewmaster. The word *castle* is courtesy. The word *brewmaster* you will have to earn.

The player takes the key.

**TITLE: OLD STABLES**  
**CHAPTER ONE: WHAT WE KEEP**

## Sequence 2 — The Cold Copper

**Same evening · Old Stables · Guided exploration**

**Image:** The stable doors fight every inch. Damp limestone, old soot and a copper kettle under gray cloth. Four lanterns enter separately, each carried by a named pair of working hands.

The key turns, but the doors do not open. JULES braces a shoulder against one while the player works the other.

**JULES**  
Lift when you push. The left hinge thinks gravity is a negotiation.

The doors groan inward.

APOLLINE has already sent four people:

- Jules clears the chimney, checks belts and finds a cracked steam line that must remain isolated.
- MAËLLE counts intact bottles and rejects half the corks.
- NOOR opens shutters, lights a kitchen range in the tack room and puts soup where everyone can reach it.
- INEZ records eighteen kilograms of pale malt from the estate granary and an empty space where hops ought to be.

**INEZ**  
The malt is dry. The mice have excellent taste but poor discipline. We have enough sound grain for one small brew and a mistake.

**BREWMASTER**  
Yeast?

**INEZ**  
A covered pitching crock is coming on the morning cart from Brasserie Saint-Odile. Fresh today, if their brewer likes us as much as the Countess believes.

**MAËLLE**  
And hops?

Inez turns the page. Nothing.

**NOOR**  
There are old vines by the millstream. Bitter cones in autumn. Mostly brown now.

**APOLLINE**  
An express purchase consumes the contract before we have the contract.

The player inspects five stations: copper, mash tun, coolship, open fermenter and packaging bench. The visual language is tactile—chalk marks, rivets, warped oak, fingers testing seams.

At the copper, the player finds an old hammered repair.

**JULES**  
Your predecessor put that patch on in 1887.

**ARMAND**  
My father called it ugly.

**JULES**  
It held.

**ARMAND**  
My father was often wrong about useful things.

**Objective:** Make the Old Stables safe enough for a twenty-litre proving batch without pretending it is restored.

The player allocates the first workday. Whatever is chosen, one defect remains visible. The brewery should feel reclaimed, not magically repaired.

At night, the five workers gather around Noor's pot. Apolline remains standing with the ledger.

**NOOR**  
Sit. Soup does not improve for being supervised.

Apolline sits.

**APOLLINE**  
Tomorrow the innkeeper from the Three Lanterns will take one cask on trial. If it is clean. If it is ready. If his customers ask for a second.

**MAËLLE**  
That is three ifs.

**APOLLINE**  
Three is a short list here.

## Sequence 3 — The First Mash

**11–19 January 1901 · Old Stables · Core brewing tutorial**

**Image:** Steam lifts into cold rafters. The player and Jules grip the same wooden mash paddle. Apolline's inked finger rests on the fuel column of the ledger. No one watches idly.

The batch is named **Lantern Blonde** after Maëlle sketches three small lanterns on a scrap of cream paper.

### Gameplay beat: heat

The old fire draws too hard. Mash temperature climbs.

**JULES**  
She has been asleep eleven years and wakes hungry.

Player response:

1. **Cut the fire and stir hard.** More labor, controlled body.
2. **Add measured cold liquor.** Lower strength, steadier conversion.
3. **Accept the heat.** Fuller, sweeter beer; higher quality risk.

Apolline asks for the consequence, not a justification.

**APOLLINE**  
What did it cost?

The player answers with the changed fuel, labor or expected yield. This is the first lesson of their relationship: she will defend a risk that has been named; she will oppose a risk disguised as confidence.

### Gameplay beat: bitterness

The purchased hops do not arrive. The player chooses:

1. **Inspect the millstream vines** and use the best remaining cones with estate herbs. Low cash cost, uncertain flavor; unlocks the estate-foraging line.
2. **Order express hops** through Cécile's contact. Reliable profile, severe runway cost; creates a social debt.
3. **Reduce bitterness** and sell honestly as a softer table beer. Lower prestige, strong margin.

If the player chooses the vines, Noor and Inez accompany him. Noor rubs a cone between her palms and rejects it.

**NOOR**  
Cheese.

**BREWMASTER**  
That is not a tasting note I want in beer.

She selects another.

**NOOR**  
This one still smells of summer.

If the player orders express hops, Cécile writes one short letter. She does not boast about the recipient.

**CÉCILE**  
A favor is not free because no invoice arrives with it.

If the player reduces bitterness, Maëlle changes the label rather than hiding the choice.

**MAËLLE**  
We sell what we brewed, not what we hoped to brew.

### Gameplay beat: fermentation and package

The pitching yeast arrives warm beneath a wool cover. The player cleans and transfers into the open fermenter. A seven-day fermentation begins.

Time passes through close shots of the same hands:

- Jules skims foam with a copper bowl.
- Inez marks gravity in the black ledger.
- Maëlle washes a small coopered transport cask.
- Noor covers bread dough with a cloth beside the gentle heat.
- Apolline moves one coin from **fuel** to **wages**, then closes the box.

On packaging day, Maëlle drives the final wooden bung.

**MAËLLE**  
One cask. Twenty litres. Enough to save the house?

**BREWMASTER**  
Enough to learn whether a second cask is worth making.

## Sequence 4 — The Night of First Lights

**20 January–17 February 1901 · Village and stable court · First contract arc**

**Image:** A horse-drawn flat brewer's wagon carries one small cask downhill. Match cut to the same cask raised onto the Three Lanterns bar by Maëlle and the innkeeper, ALPHONSE MAROT.

At the Three Lanterns, Alphonse draws the first cloudy glass. He gives it to the oldest regular, not the Count.

The regular drinks, thinks too long, then taps the rim for another.

**ALPHONSE**  
That is not praise. That is an order.

The first sale unlocks two Week Two contracts:

- **Saint Brigid's Chapel Supper:** fifty guests, Lantern Blonde, meaningful advance, community visibility.
- **The Count's Cellar Reserve:** thirty-two guests, Stable Amber, higher quality demand, less immediate cash.

Armand expects the reserve. Apolline recommends the supper because its advance covers wages. Cécile refuses to tell the player which relationship matters more.

**CÉCILE**  
If I choose every guest for you, they remain my guests.

The selected contract plays as the current second-brew challenge. The amber route includes the runoff choice: protect strength, extend volume or blend carefully. The festival route stresses deadline, staffing and service.

In Week Three, both the Abbey winter table and the Three Lanterns cellar request beer. Malt, casks, labor, cash and the single usable fermenter cannot satisfy everything. The player must decline, delay or renegotiate at least one request.

If the brewery reaches insolvency, Apolline secures a small emergency note and puts the player on probation.

**APOLLINE**  
This is not rescue. It is one more chance to produce evidence.

If the brewery remains solvent, she sets the note she prepared into the fire without comment.

### The courtyard service

Whatever route the player chose, the act culminates in the restored loading court. Lanterns are hung from repaired stable hooks. Workers, villagers and a few well-dressed guests share long tables. The visual priority is named people serving one another: Maëlle on the cask tap, Noor passing bowls, Jules relighting a lantern, Inez counting returned cups.

Amaury and Éléonore do not yet exist. The empty places beside Apolline matter.

Armand lifts a glass.

**ARMAND**  
To Valenne beer.

The player may answer:

1. **“To the Old Stables.”** — Restoration gain.
2. **“To those who made it.”** — Community gain.
3. **“To the next batch.”** — Count confidence gain.

The crowd chooses the player's words over Armand's. He notices.

### First council

After the tables empty, Armand, Cécile, Apolline and the player meet around the brewhouse workbench. The player allocates the first surplus:

- restore stable lighting;
- protect the runway and pay a creditor;
- support the village inn and next community service.

**APOLLINE**  
One successful month does not make an institution.

**BREWMASTER**  
No. Repetition does.

Armand places a smaller iron key beside the black stable key.

**ARMAND**  
Then repeat it. Keeper of the Old Stables.

If promotion thresholds were not met, he keeps the smaller key and says:

**ARMAND**  
Earn the second key.

The title card does not change history; it changes the player's authority during the next seasons.

**MATCH CUT:** The repaired copper patch, wet in 1901. The same patch, polished by use in 1902.

# ACT II — THE PRICE OF BELONGING

## Sequence 5 — Black Bread

**15 April 1902 · Old Stables yard · Labor crisis**

**Historical pressure:** A national general strike for equal male suffrage has stopped factories and transport. Violence elsewhere has made every rumor sharper. The fictional Valenne district remains rural, but quarrymen, railway hands and brewery carters have kin in the industrial towns.

**Image:** No production smoke. Men and women stand at the gates with folded arms. Noor's hands slice the last dark loaves while Apolline's hand holds the cashbox shut.

BAPTISTE LEROUX, a quarry worker and regular at the Three Lanterns, asks to use the brewery court for a meeting. The district commissioner has warned Armand that any assembly may be treated as agitation.

**ARMAND**  
I can request restraint. I cannot order it.

**BAPTISTE**  
Your request reaches the commissioner. Mine reaches the constable's stick.

**APOLLINE**  
If the railway remains stopped, our casks do not move. If our casks do not move, wages stop next week whether we approve of the strike or not.

**MAËLLE**  
Then say that to them. Do not make me say it after the gate is barred.

Major choice:

1. **Open the court and kitchen.** Suspend brewing, serve bread and low-strength table beer, allow a peaceful meeting. Large community gain, lost production, commissioner suspicion.
2. **Keep the brewery working with volunteers.** Protect cash and contracts; pay a risk bonus. Count confidence rises, but community trust depends on how workers are treated.
3. **Broker two hours.** Armand asks the commissioner to remain outside; Baptiste guarantees no weapons; production resumes after the meeting. Requires earlier Count confidence and community trust.

The player's key dialogue is selected from tone options but retains the same meaning:

**BREWMASTER**  
No one enters as the Count's subject or leaves as my employee. In this yard, each person answers for his own conduct.

Apolline looks at the grain store.

**APOLLINE**  
Neutrality is easiest for the person holding the bread.

If the player opened the kitchen, he gives her the store key.

**BREWMASTER**  
Then you decide how much neutrality we can afford.

She opens it.

The meeting does not settle the national conflict. It does something smaller and more useful: nobody in the Valenne court is beaten, and the workers return knowing exactly what the brewery did when choosing became expensive.

Later, Armand receives a terse note from the commissioner.

**ARMAND**  
He says my name has been entered in a report.

**CÉCILE**  
Then at last the name has done a day's work.

**Inheritance:** The yard becomes known as a place where hostile groups can speak. That reputation returns in 1913 and again in 1914.

## Sequence 6 — The Crushed Hand

**3 October 1903 · Packaging room · Safety and liability**

**Image:** A drive belt lashes loose. Jules' bare hand reaches for the emergency lever while apprentice LUC MARTIN shields his face. The frame cuts before impact.

A refurbished belt slips from the packaging shaft. The player has seconds to stop the line. The mechanical interaction tests whether prior maintenance was funded.

Luc survives, but two fingers are crushed. The doctor says he may never set hoops or lift full casks safely again.

In the tack-room kitchen, Luc's mother waits. Apolline lays out wages, treatment and the debt installment. Only two can be paid in full.

**APOLLINE**  
The law will decide what an employer owes after it has finished debating. His hand cannot wait for Parliament.

**JULES**  
The guard was on my list.

**BREWMASTER**  
And the lamp. And the flue. And the south drain.

**JULES**  
That does not give the boy his fingers.

Choice:

1. **Pay treatment and retain Luc on lighter work.** Cash loss; unlocks Luc as records clerk and later bottling supervisor.
2. **Pay the legal minimum and hire a replacement.** Runway protected; severe community loss.
3. **Armand advances the money personally.** Available if confidence is high; creates a debt to the family and leaves the system unsafe unless the player still funds guards.

The strongest path does not restore Luc's old life. He teaches himself to write with his left hand and starts copying delivery returns into the black ledger.

**LUC**  
I used to know a cask by lifting it.

**INEZ**  
Now you will know where it went, whether it came back, and who lies about breaking the tap. This is more power than lifting.

**Inheritance:** Safety becomes a visible production system. Accidents are not random melodrama; deferred maintenance and overwork increase their probability.

## Sequence 7 — The Patent Supper

**12 November 1904 · Château dining room and brewhouse · Dynastic bargain**

**Image:** Two documents lie side by side: the brewery incorporation papers, spotted with workroom ink, and a cream copy of a royal regrant tied in faded red. Between them is the black stable key.

The brewery now pays regular wages and services part of the estate debt, but the Valenne title still has no direct heir. Cécile's relationships have secured consideration of an exceptional regrant. It comes with no public office, land or money. It preserves only a name and a title—and only if Apolline marries LAURENT DE RIVECOURT and their children assume the Valenne name.

Laurent is educated, courteous and genuinely impressed by the brewhouse. He also believes inherited hierarchy is a stabilizing technology.

Before supper, he meets the player beside the copper.

**LAURENT**  
They say you saved Valenne.

**BREWMASTER**  
People say that because kettles make poor portraits.

**LAURENT**  
And yet someone must own the kettle.

**BREWMASTER**  
Someone must clean it first.

Upstairs, Apolline refuses to sign the marriage settlement unless the brewery is placed in a distinct operating company and her administrative role is written into its governing agreement to the fullest extent the period permits.

**APOLLINE**  
I will not save the Valenne name by becoming unable to sign for Valenne malt.

**LAURENT**  
The law makes a husband answerable.

**APOLLINE**  
The law makes a wife disappear into the answer.

Laurent studies the agreement. Cécile does not rescue him from the silence.

**LAURENT**  
Then we make the answer explicit. You administer. I countersign where the law insists, not where pride does.

He signs. This does not make the legal inequality vanish; it reveals the terms on which Apolline is willing to live inside it.

### Player contribution: the wedding beer

The player designs one brew from the same wort for château and village service. Presentation can differ, but the liquid cannot.

- A refined pale version impresses court contacts.
- A deep Stable Amber emphasizes house tradition.
- A herb-led seasonal beer emphasizes local independence.

At the supper, Victor Sarlet of the Compagnie Brassicole des Trois Provinces tastes it.

**SARLET**  
Very good. Difficult to repeat, I imagine.

**MAËLLE**  
We intend to disappoint you.

The regrant is read privately. Its relevant line establishes that Apolline's eldest legitimate son, bearing the Valenne name and arms, will stand as heir to Armand's title. A daughter is not included.

The player notices Apolline pause at the masculine wording.

**APOLLINE**  
We have preserved an answer for a child who does not exist.

**CÉCILE**  
That is what families call planning.

**APOLLINE**  
And breweries call speculation.

**Inheritance:** The title's future has been secured, but title succession and brewery control are now visibly separate. The distinction will make the later conflict between Amaury and Éléonore possible.

## Sequence 8 — A Day Belongs to a Person

**17 July–19 December 1905 · Old Stables · Labor charter and birth of the heir**

**Historical pressure:** Belgium's Sunday-rest law forces industrial and commercial employers to confront weekly rest, with practical exceptions for work that cannot simply stop.

The brewery cannot abandon an active fermentation because a calendar changes. It can stop treating every emergency as an excuse to demand every worker.

At a workbench council, Jules asks for rotating duty and a full substitute rest day after Sunday cellar work. Apolline first calculates the added headcount. Laurent argues that a household has always required service when required.

**NOOR**  
A fire also requires service. We do not therefore set the kitchen alight each Sunday.

Player policy choice:

1. **Rotating cellar watch with paid substitute rest.** Higher labor cost, fewer accidents, high trust.
2. **Sunday shutdown except declared fermentation emergencies.** Lower cost, production risk, strong discipline.
3. **Family-and-volunteer coverage.** Cheap initially, resentment if overused.

The policy is chalked on the wall in names, not anonymous shifts.

**MAËLLE**  
If the work is important enough to take a person's Sunday, it is important enough to write down whose Sunday it took.

In December, church bells ring before dawn. Apolline gives birth to a healthy son in the château. Laurent crosses the yard to tell the night crew.

**LAURENT**  
Amaury Armand de Valenne. The line is secure.

Armand opens a bottle of the 1904 wedding beer. Cécile takes it from him before he can pour.

**CÉCILE**  
Not that one. It is the last.

**ARMAND**  
That is why bottles open.

They share it in the brewhouse rather than the drawing room.

Armand raises his glass toward the château.

**ARMAND**  
To the next Count de Valenne.

Jules raises his toward the fermenter.

**JULES**  
And to the poor devil who has cellar watch.

The laughter is affectionate but important. Amaury is celebrated as heir before he can choose anything. The brewery crew immediately returns to work that no title can perform.

**MATCH CUT:** A drop of beer runs down Armand's glass. Three years later, rain runs down the nursery window.

# ACT III — CHILDREN OF THE HOUSE

## Sequence 9 — The Second Child

**8 March 1908 · Château nursery and Old Stables · Capacity crisis**

**Image:** A baby's hand closes around Apolline's ink-stained finger. Below, a sight glass clouds with spoiled beer. Joy and operational failure occupy the same morning.

ÉLÉONORE DE VALENNE is born during the brewery's largest contracted week. She is healthy; Apolline is not in danger, but she will be confined for weeks. Laurent assumes that routine signatures and supplier decisions can wait.

They cannot.

The cold cellar rises three degrees after an ice delivery fails. One fermenter shows a sharp medicinal taint. The player must decide whether to dump the batch before an inspector, innkeeper or family member tells him to.

**MAËLLE**  
It is forty-eight casks promised under our name.

**BREWMASTER**  
It is forty-eight casks that cannot leave under our name.

If the player hides or blends the tainted beer, the short-term contract survives but the truth emerges later through Alphonse at the Three Lanterns. If he dumps it, runway suffers and Sarlet arrives with an offer of refrigerated capacity from the Compagnie. If the player isolates and salvages only verified sound casks, success depends on prior records and Luc's traceability work.

Luc places the black ledger on the table and points with the two good fingers of his right hand.

**LUC**  
These six were filled before the temperature rose. Those twelve after. The rest were in the south room.

**INEZ**  
You know that?

**LUC**  
I wrote it. That is the purpose of writing.

The incident proves that the brewery can function while Apolline is absent—but only because she and the player built procedures other people can use.

Three weeks later she enters the brewhouse carrying Éléonore. Amaury, two years old, walks beside Laurent holding a tiny silver-topped cane made for a formal portrait.

Armand dips one finger in clean brewing liquor and touches it to Éléonore's forehead.

**APOLLINE**  
Papa, she has been baptized.

**ARMAND**  
This is an inspection.

Amaury reaches for the black stable key at the player's belt.

**LAURENT**  
One day that will all be yours.

Apolline looks from the key to the brewery crew.

**APOLLINE**  
No. One day much will be entrusted to him. There is a difference.

Laurent hears the correction. Amaury does not.

**Inheritance:** Éléonore's birth brings no title celebration. It does reveal that the procedures of the brewery—not one indispensable hero—allow the institution to outlast absence, illness and error.

## Sequence 10 — Sarlet's Cold Promise

**6 September 1908 · Old Stables council · Modernization choice**

**Image:** Victor Sarlet's clean technical drawings cover the scarred workbench. His white glove rests beside Jules' blackened hand. The proposed machinery is beautiful because it solves real problems.

Sarlet offers three things the Old Stables cannot easily provide alone: a dependable ice contract, machine-made bottles at scale and access to the Compagnie's rail distribution. In return, the Old Stables must accept a long exclusive agreement and standardize two recipes to the Compagnie's targets.

**SARLET**  
You call variation character when customers forgive it. When they do not, they call it bad beer.

**BREWMASTER**  
Consistency is not the same as sameness.

**SARLET**  
That distinction becomes expensive at five thousand bottles.

He is right about the quality risk and wrong that only scale can solve it.

Strategic choice:

1. **Build an icehouse and improve cellar insulation.** Preserves top-fermented identity and independence; high construction cost; strong restoration.
2. **Buy a small steam pump and bottle rinser.** Raises throughput and safety; does not solve summer temperature.
3. **Sign limited rail distribution.** Immediate market growth; the Compagnie gains leverage over price and brand presentation.
4. **Take Sarlet's full package.** Powerful growth path; recipe constraints and long-term dependency.

Apolline favors any choice whose obligations can be priced and terminated. Armand dislikes the Compagnie touching the Valenne label. Cécile asks the decisive question.

**CÉCILE**  
If he disappears tomorrow, which part can we still operate with our own hands?

The resulting equipment becomes permanent scenery. It is never a glowing upgrade that erases the old room. New pipework bends around stone mangers; the old copper patch remains visible.

At the end of the council, Sarlet sees the 1887 patch.

**SARLET**  
At our works, that kettle would be scrap.

**JULES**  
Here it is employed.

## Sequence 11 — One Son from Every House

**19 December 1909 · Loading court · Conscription**

**Historical pressure:** Belgium has replaced the old lottery-and-substitution system with a rule drawing one son from each family. The army still feels distant, but military obligation now crosses the same household boundaries as brewery labor.

BASTIEN, Jules' nineteen-year-old nephew and the brewery's fastest cellarman, receives his notice. Jules asks Armand to use his acquaintances to have the posting changed.

**JULES**  
You know colonels.

**ARMAND**  
I know men who dine with colonels. That is not the same as commanding one.

**JULES**  
It may be the same to a boy sleeping in barracks.

Laurent believes a Valenne request could secure Bastien a nearby supply posting. Apolline points out that another family's son would then take the distant place.

Player choice:

1. **Use the connection.** Bastien remains close and the brewery keeps skill; the favor costs Armand standing and troubles community trust.
2. **Let the posting stand and guarantee Bastien's job.** Lose labor now; gain loyalty and a trained returning worker later.
3. **Ask only for a brewing/supply assignment based on competence.** A fairer request with uncertain result; requires records proving Bastien's role.

Whichever route is taken, Bastien packs. Maëlle gives him a bottle wrapped in straw.

**BASTIEN**  
Will it keep?

**MAËLLE**  
Longer than you think. Not forever.

Young Amaury, almost four, watches the uniformed recruiting sergeant cross the yard.

**AMAURY**  
Did he salute Grandpapa?

**LAURENT**  
He should have.

Éléonore, not yet two, has both hands in a basket of returned bottle stoppers.

**CÉCILE**  
The boy noticed the salute. The girl noticed what came back.

**APOLLINE**  
They are children, Maman.

**CÉCILE**  
Yes. That is when noticing begins.

**Inheritance:** The brewery begins a guarantee that returning workers will recover their place or an equivalent wage. It is costly in peace and foundational after war.

# ACT IV — THE BRIGHT YEARS

## Sequence 12 — Thirteen Million Eyes

**14–16 August 1910 · Brussels International Exposition / Old Stables · Expansion climax**

**Historical pressure:** The Brussels International Exposition displays modern industry to millions. On the night of 14–15 August, a real fire destroys multiple pavilions without loss of life but with immense material damage.

**Image:** Electric exposition light reflected in a bottle of Stable Amber. Match cut to orange fire reflected in the same glass.

Cécile uses a committee relationship to secure the Old Stables a shelf—not a grand pavilion—within a regional producers' display. The opportunity requires hundreds of bottles, a reliable rail shipment and labels that city buyers can read at a glance.

For the first time, the player designs a production plan months ahead:

- protect local contracts or divert stock to Brussels;
- present Lantern Blonde, Stable Amber or a new strong château beer;
- accept Maëlle's clear cream-and-ink label or Armand's heraldic design;
- ship through Sarlet's network or coordinate independent carts and rail freight.

At the exposition, an industrial brewer demonstrates artificial cooling and bright, repeatable lager. The Old Stables product is not treated as automatically superior because it is old.

**CITY BUYER**  
Can you make every bottle taste like this one?

**BREWMASTER**  
I can make every sound bottle belong to the same beer.

**CITY BUYER**  
That was not my question.

Before the player can close the largest order, fire tears through the exposition grounds. There is no heroic rescue inside the flames. People are evacuated. Stock, display materials and signed order slips are lost.

The next morning, bottles lie burst in ash. Maëlle finds the carved Three Lanterns printing block, scorched along one edge.

**MAËLLE**  
The labels are gone.

**BREWMASTER**  
The block remains.

Sarlet offers to reproduce the lost order through the Compagnie's facilities—if the Old Stables signs over exclusive city distribution for ten years.

Choice:

1. **Accept the exclusive deal.** Recovers revenue quickly; creates durable dependency.
2. **Return home and rebuild the orders slowly.** Preserves independence; severe cash hit; local partners may bridge the gap if community trust is high.
3. **Form a small producers' cooperative.** Requires relationships from previous contracts; slower growth, shared distribution assets.

Countess Cécile is the one who reframes the apparent failure.

**CÉCILE**  
We came to be seen. We have been. Now they will remember whether we kept our word after the spectacle burned.

### Time-lapse: 1910–1912

The player repeats and improves operations through two compressed seasons:

- orders are recopied from Luc's ledger;
- the scorched woodblock prints a revised label with one dark edge left visible;
- bottles join casks, but deposits and returns remain part of the economy;
- the icehouse, pump, rinser or distributor chosen earlier proves its value and exact limitation;
- estate grain contracts become planned rather than scavenged;
- Noor's kitchen becomes a permanent workers' meal room;
- Apolline adds brewery results to the estate council instead of pleading for its budget.

The château roof is repaired with brewery income. Armand watches the scaffolding from the stable court.

**ARMAND**  
The house shelters the brewery again.

**APOLLINE**  
No, Papa. The brewery has paid for the house to shelter anything at all.

He takes the correction without offense.

## Sequence 13 — The Heir's Measure

**5 October 1912 · Old Stables annual open day · Character inheritance**

**Image:** Amaury's clean glove rests on a cask head bearing the crest. Éléonore's small bare hand is stained with charcoal from Luc's tally board. Between them, the player rolls the cask rather than posing beside it.

The brewery holds its first annual open day for suppliers, workers' families, innkeepers and estate tenants. It is the liveliest the Old Stables has ever been. Every conversation is attached to real operations: a cooper demonstrates hoops; Inez explains malt grades; Maëlle runs bottle returns; Noor serves stew made from the same grain economy that feeds the brewhouse.

Amaury, nearly seven, has been taught that he will be Count. He is not cruel. He is observant, charming and accustomed to adults turning his mistakes into jokes.

He stamps the Valenne crest into a damp label, dislikes the result and orders LUC to make another.

**LUC**  
Ask, Monsieur Amaury.

**AMAURY**  
It will be mine.

The room stills. Laurent prepares to smooth over the moment. The player receives a response choice:

1. **“The title may be yours. Luc's hand is not.”** Direct; Amaury resentment, worker trust.
2. **“Then learn to make the label.”** Practical; unlocks a hands-on lesson.
3. **Ask Armand to answer.** Tests the Count's growth and previous confidence.

On a mature Armand route, the Count kneels—slowly now—to Amaury's height.

**ARMAND**  
The title comes without asking. That is why it proves nothing.

Amaury flushes. He apologizes because he has been made to, not because he understands.

Later, the player finds Éléonore, four, arranging returned stoppers into full and broken piles beside Inez.

**ÉLÉONORE**  
The broken ones still have the mark.

**INEZ**  
A mark tells you who owned a thing. It does not make the thing useful.

Éléonore pockets one broken stopper. This is not proof that she is destined or naturally perfect. It is the first object she chooses to keep because damage does not erase meaning.

Apolline watches both children from across the room.

**APOLLINE**  
We have taught one what he receives and the other what must be counted.

**BREWMASTER**  
We can still teach both the rest.

## Sequence 14 — Ten Days in April

**14–24 April 1913 · Old Stables and railway siding · National strike**

**Historical pressure:** A carefully organized general strike seeks electoral reform and the end of plural voting. Hundreds of thousands participate. Rail movement and industrial orders tighten around the brewery.

**Image:** The work schedule and a strike notice share the same nail. A steam whistle remains silent. On the court tables, glasses are inverted so the gathering cannot be dismissed as a drinking crowd.

Baptiste returns, older and better organized. Brewery workers ask for a paid day to attend a district assembly. Sarlet warns that a missed rail shipment will trigger the Old Stables' penalty clause. Laurent argues that an employer cannot finance a strike against the social order that protects the estate.

**BAPTISTE**  
Your Count has more than one vote. Jules has one. Noor has none. Yet all three can be ruined by the same invoice.

**ARMAND**  
My votes have not repaired a roof in years.

**BAPTISTE**  
Then you will not miss them.

Armand is stung because the answer is fair.

Strategic choice:

1. **Close for the assembly and pay the day.** Lose shipment and cash; major trust gain; Sarlet applies contractual pressure.
2. **Run a volunteer skeleton shift and release everyone else.** Protect fermentation and some orders; outcome depends on the 1905 duty policy.
3. **Renegotiate the shipment as a cooperative bloc.** Available if the 1910 cooperative exists; spreads the penalty and creates regional solidarity.
4. **Forbid absence.** Protect near-term control; risks a walkout and permanently changes the 1914 response.

The court meeting is peaceful. The brewery does not “solve” suffrage. It provides tables, drinking water, food and a place to record names of absent workers so families know where they are.

During the debate, Amaury repeats something he heard from Laurent.

**AMAURY**  
Grandpapa should tell them to work.

**ÉLÉONORE**  
They know he cannot.

She is five. The line is not delivered as a triumph; she simply learned the limit in 1902 from a story Cécile tells.

After ten days the strike ends with a promise of political examination, not immediate equality. The brewery resumes. Every delayed cask must now be triaged, and players face the economic consequence of whichever moral position they took.

Sarlet arrives to enforce or renegotiate the penalty.

**SARLET**  
You keep turning your brewhouse into a public square.

**BREWMASTER**  
Only when the public square has no roof.

**SARLET**  
Roofs are liabilities unless someone charges rent.

**APOLLINE**  
And empty roofs are ruins. We have experience of those.

## Sequence 15 — The Four Inheritances

**21 December 1913 · Weekly council · Stewardship resolution**

**Image:** Four items in a square on the council table: the red-tied patent, a map of the estate, brewery share papers, and the ring of working keys. Four different hands reach toward them, then stop.

The estate has survived twelve years. It is not wealthy. It is solvent enough to plan, employed enough to matter and trusted enough that its failure would injure people beyond the Valenne household.

Apolline presents a family compact and operating charter:

1. **The title** passes under the royal regrant to Amaury when Armand dies.
2. **The château and land** pass under the family's wills and settlements, burdened by their debts and duties.
3. **Brewery shares** remain a distinct asset. They may be divided, inherited or transferred under the company agreement.
4. **The office of Estate Steward** is appointed for competence and may be removed for failure. It is not a courtesy attached to blood.

Laurent dislikes the implied possibility that a future Count might not control daily operations.

**LAURENT**  
You are constructing a house in which the master may be refused his own key.

**APOLLINE**  
I am constructing a brewery in which an untrained owner cannot poison his own customers.

**ARMAND**  
And I am old enough to know that the two buildings need not obey the same rules.

The Count turns to the player.

If the player has already earned Deputy Steward, the appointment is direct. Otherwise, strong community or restoration performance can support a conditional appointment; weak performance retains Apolline as acting administrator and makes the player Steward of Brewery Operations until the first wartime test.

**ARMAND**  
In 1901 I gave you the key because I had run out of things to sell. You returned it to me attached to work, wages and obligations. I appoint you Estate Steward—not above Apolline, not in place of this family, but responsible to both.

Apolline takes the key ring first. She removes the black stable key and hands the remaining estate keys to the player.

**APOLLINE**  
The brewery key stays where it has always belonged.

She fastens it to the player's ring last.

**APOLLINE**  
With the person who can account for what happens after the door opens.

The UI promotion appears: **ESTATE STEWARD**.

Player acceptance:

1. **“I serve the estate.”** — Count confidence.
2. **“I answer for the work.”** — Restoration.
3. **“I will keep the doors open.”** — Community trust; becomes the line recalled in 1914.

Éléonore watches the keys. Amaury watches the signet ring on Armand's hand.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

The sound of a full courtyard in summer.

# ACT V — THE LAST PEACE

## Sequence 16 — The Summer Photograph

**28 June 1914 · Old Stables courtyard · False ending**

**Image:** The entire institution arranged for a photograph, but nobody can stay still. A horse tosses its head. A worker wipes foam from a tap. Éléonore grips the scorched label block; Amaury wears Armand's old ceremonial sash. The copper glows through the open doors behind them.

The Old Stables holds a midsummer open day celebrating thirteen years of continuous operation. The player reviews the inherited result of the chapter:

- which equipment was restored;
- whether the Compagnie controls distribution;
- whether Luc became records supervisor;
- whether Bastien returned from service;
- which inns, abbey buyers and village groups trust the brewery;
- how much debt still burdens the estate;
- which beer became the house signature.

This is a playable festival, not a victory cinematic. Orders must be served, bottle deposits counted and a leaking cask managed while guests circulate.

A photographer from the district paper arranges the Valennes in front. Armand calls the player and brewery crew into the frame.

**PHOTOGRAPHER**  
The household first, Monsieur le Comte. Staff afterward.

**ARMAND**  
You are photographing the brewery. Arrange the people who make it.

Laurent stands at Apolline's side. Amaury stands before them because he is the heir. Éléonore slips from position to return a dropped stopper to Maëlle.

The shutter opens.

At that instant the church bell begins an unscheduled peal. A newspaper boy enters the court calling news from Sarajevo: the Austrian Archduke and his wife have been killed.

Conversation dips, then resumes. Europe has produced crises before. The day's beer still needs serving.

Sarlet reads the paper alone.

**SARLET**  
If Vienna moves against Serbia, rail priority will change before politicians admit why.

**APOLLINE**  
You can make a contract crisis out of an assassination.

**SARLET**  
That is why my contracts survive crises.

The player chooses one final peacetime investment: extra malt, spare casks, debt repayment, or a new delivery horse. Every option becomes ironic but useful in a different way six weeks later.

At dusk, Armand and the player stand in the doorway.

**ARMAND**  
Thirteen years ago I thought success would feel like being restored to the life we had.

**BREWMASTER**  
Does it?

**ARMAND**  
No. Thank God.

This is the emotional completion of the founding story. The next scenes change its meaning.

## Sequence 17 — The Notice Board

**31 July 1914 · Village square and brewery · Mobilization**

**Image:** Official notices are pasted over a beer poster. One brush smooths the mobilization paper while another hand tears brewery shifts from the schedule.

Belgian mobilization reaches the district. Men crowd the square to find their classes and units. The brewery whistle calls no shift; it calls households to hear names.

Laurent holds a reserve officer's commission. Bastien, if present, is recalled. Several carters and malt workers must report. The player, forty-two, remains with the estate, but the labor plan collapses in minutes.

**LAURENT**  
It may be precaution. Neutrality has held before.

**APOLLINE**  
Then go as a precaution and come home as one.

She fixes one of his collar tabs. Her hand shakes only after she lets go.

Amaury is eight. He is excited by the uniform until he sees Apolline's face.

**AMAURY**  
Will Papa command?

**LAURENT**  
I will obey first. Remember that.

This is Laurent's clearest attempt to correct what he has taught his son.

At the Old Stables, the player converts the capacity board:

- brewing staff available;
- workers mobilized;
- horses and carts subject to requisition;
- malt, clean water, fuel and food on hand;
- casks at inns that can be recalled;
- families dependent on wages.

The last delivery horse is led out for military inspection. The composition mirrors the bailiff taking the carriage horse in 1901.

**JULES**  
We began without a horse.

**BREWMASTER**  
Then we remember how.

If the player bought a new horse in June, losing it hurts logistics. If he stocked malt, the stores are strong. If he repaid debt, credit is available. If he bought casks, water and food can be moved. No peacetime choice becomes simply “wrong.”

## Sequence 18 — The Ultimatum

**2–3 August 1914 · Château office · Final council**

**Image:** The red-tied Valenne patent, the black brewery ledger and a telegram form a triangle beneath one lamp. Outside, the courtyard is full of people waiting for information.

A telegram reports Germany's demand for passage through neutral Belgium. A second message confirms the Belgian refusal.

Armand wears formal evening clothes though no guest is expected. Cécile is already writing to families in Liège, Brussels and Namur, using every relationship she has maintained. She asks for road reports, hospital contacts, carts and places for children—not military influence.

**CÉCILE**  
A relation cannot stop an army. It can tell you which bridge is still open.

Apolline puts the family patent and marriage settlement into a dispatch case. Then she places the brewery share register beside them and hesitates.

**APOLLINE**  
Which papers do we send away?

Armand touches the patent.

**ARMAND**  
That proves what we were granted.

The player touches the black ledger.

**BREWMASTER**  
This proves who is owed wages, who has our casks, what grain is clean, and what can feed people tomorrow.

The emergency choice determines which archive is physically protected off-site:

1. **Send the patent and family papers.** Protect dynastic legitimacy; brewery documentation remains at risk.
2. **Send the operational ledgers and recipes.** Protect continuity; the Valenne legal archive remains at the château.
3. **Split the packet through two trusted routes.** Available only if the player maintained both noble and community relationships; neither packet is complete, but no single loss destroys everything.

Amaury asks why the crest is being packed.

**AMAURY**  
They cannot take the name.

**ÉLÉONORE**  
They took the horse.

No adult has an answer that satisfies either child.

Armand removes his signet ring but does not give it to Amaury. He seals Cécile's letters instead.

**ARMAND**  
A title is not a weapon. Tonight it is postage.

## Sequence 19 — Open the Doors

**4 August 1914, before dawn · Old Stables · Chapter finale**

**Image:** The same stable doors as Sequence 2. This time they open easily. Beyond them are village families, carts from the eastern road and the first exhausted travelers. Lantern light touches named faces and working hands. Far away, a dull sound may be thunder or guns.

News arrives that German troops have crossed the frontier.

The château bell rings. People converge on the estate because the court was a meeting place in 1902 and 1913, because Noor has fed them, because Maëlle knows every inn road, because Inez can count stores, and because the brewery owns vessels meant to hold and move liquid safely.

Apolline and the player stand before the capacity board for the last command phase of the chapter.

**INEZ**  
Malt for three ordinary brews. Four if we lower strength. Fuel for nine days. Two sound carts, no horse. Thirty-one casks on site, twelve in the village.

**NOOR**  
The kitchen can feed sixty this morning. After that, it depends who brings what.

**MAËLLE**  
The eastern innkeepers are sending families west. They need water more than beer.

**JULES**  
The copper can boil both.

Final operational choice:

1. **Suspend sale beer and turn capacity to boiled water, food and low-strength table beer.** Immediate community strength; no commercial revenue.
2. **Keep one commercial brew running while using the kitchen and spare copper for relief.** Protects wage continuity; stretches labor.
3. **Secure the plant and conserve all stores for the estate.** Strong supplies; only sustainable if community trust was already low, and it changes who remains loyal in occupation.

Armand enters carrying the remaining estate keys. His age is suddenly visible.

**ARMAND**  
Steward.

The word is not ceremony now.

**ARMAND**  
What does the house require?

Player response reflects the 1913 acceptance line:

- **“The estate serves the people inside it.”**
- **“The work comes first.”**
- **“We keep the doors open.”**

Armand nods and follows the player's instruction. This completes his arc: the Count's final act in the chapter is not command but competent service.

A frightened cart horse shies outside. Éléonore runs after the black stable key when it slips from the player's ring. Amaury starts to order her back, then stops when Armand looks at him.

Éléonore returns the key to the player.

**ÉLÉONORE**  
You dropped Valenne.

The player closes her fingers around the key, then opens them and takes it.

**BREWMASTER**  
No. Only the key.

He unlocks the grain store.

The camera rises through the stable rafters. Below, the room organizes itself:

- Noor assigns bowls and blankets.
- Inez records every sack opened.
- Maëlle pins road reports over old beer orders.
- Luc opens the black ledger to a clean page.
- Jules lights the copper.
- Cécile's letters leave by bicycle.
- Apolline stands at the center, one hand on Amaury's shoulder and the other on Éléonore's.
- Armand carries a bench with two village men.

The first flame catches beneath the old copper. It illuminates the 1887 patch.

**LUC**  
What do I call the new account?

The player looks at the open doors.

**BREWMASTER**  
Write: “Fourth of August. The brewery remains open.”

**CUT TO BLACK.**

Over darkness: the copper begins to rumble.

# Chapter outcome ledger

At the 1914 handoff, the save records:

- the player's brewing background and final authority;
- signature beer and recipe decisions;
- the 1887 copper patch and all installed equipment;
- debt, cash, malt, fuel, casks and distribution obligations;
- whether Luc was retained and whether reliable batch records exist;
- the brewery's safety and weekly-rest policies;
- Bastien's service history and promised return position;
- the 1902 and 1913 labor decisions;
- relationship strength with Armand, Cécile, Apolline, Laurent and Sarlet;
- community groups and inns that answer the 1914 call;
- the archive packet protected in the ultimatum sequence;
- whether the final operation prioritizes relief, mixed continuity or estate conservation.

The heir outcomes are invariant but not resolved:

- **Amaury** is legally positioned to become Count. He has received love, duty and repeated social permission to expect obedience.
- **Éléonore** has no automatic claim to the title. She has learned the brewery's people, objects and limits. Her possible future succession is to the institution first; any later title would require a separate, historically grounded legal act.
- **Apolline** remains head of family administration and brewery governance.
- **The player** is Estate Steward, not a Valenne and not the owner of other people's heritage. His authority is real because it is earned, bounded and documented.

# Historical grounding and fiction boundary

The local incidents, people, companies, contracts, title regrant and Château de Valenne are fictional. The following pressures are historical anchors:

- Belgium had roughly 3,200–3,300 breweries around 1900, making a small revived brewery plausible while industrial scale and new technology were already changing competition. [VISITFLANDERS beer heritage](https://www.visitflanders.com/en/discover-flanders/culinary-treats-and-belgian-beer/our-belgian-beer-culture/belgian-beer-heritage), [Oxford Academic brewing history](https://academic.oup.com/book/36747/chapter-abstract/321840270), [economic history paper](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/74982/1/dp271.pdf)
- Steam, artificial refrigeration, improved glass production and scientific control transformed brewing but demanded capital many small breweries did not have. [Visit Bruges beer history](https://www.visitbruges.be/en/eat-and-sleep/beer/history-of-beer)
- The April 1902 suffrage strike involved serious national violence; the Old Stables' peaceful local meeting is fictional and intentionally does not resolve the national struggle. [U.S. diplomatic report from 1902](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d72), [Belgian Senate voting history](https://www.senate.be/home/sections/geschiedenis_en_erfgoed/AES-SU/art-3-6_nl.html)
- Belgium enacted a Sunday-rest law in July 1905, with exceptions and later disputes over implementation. [Text of the 1905 law](https://refli.be/fr/lex/1905071751), [historical study of the law](https://www.journalbelgianhistory.be/en/system/files/article_pdf/BTNG-RBHC%2C%2032%2C%202002%2C%201-2%2C%20pp%2073-118.pdf)
- The Brussels International Exposition drew an enormous public and suffered a destructive fire on the night of 14–15 August 1910; the Valenne display and lost orders are fictional. [Université libre de Bruxelles exposition history](https://expo1910.ulb.ac.be/un-nouveau-regard/?lang=en)
- The 1913 general strike ran from 14 to 24 April and involved hundreds of thousands seeking electoral reform. [Journal of Belgian History](https://www.journalbelgianhistory.be/nl/system/files/article_pdf/BTNG-RBHC%2C%2030%2C%202000%2C%201-2%2C%20pp%20227-304.pdf)
- Belgium moved to one-son-per-family military service in 1909 and general conscription in 1913. The fictional staffing consequences make that policy operational rather than decorative. [1914–1918 Online](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/civilian-and-military-power-belgium/)
- Germany delivered its ultimatum demanding passage on 2 August 1914; Belgium refused, and German troops invaded on 4 August. [Belgian federal history of the monarchy and First World War](https://www.belgium.be/sites/default/files/downloads/280410_monarchie_EN.pdf)
- Belgian noble titles are matters of official grant and patent, not governmental power. The Valenne regrant and its succession clause are fictional devices and must receive a specialist Belgian legal-historical review before final production. [FPS Foreign Affairs overview of nobility](https://diplomatie.belgium.be/en/protocol/nobility-and-honorary-distinctions/nobility)
- The marriage negotiation reflects the restricted contractual position of married Belgian women under the period's civil law. The exact Valenne company agreement is fictional and should also receive specialist review. [Vrije Universiteit Brussel legal-history research](https://researchportal.vub.be/nl/publications/bending-the-code-civil-married-women-their-capacity-to-engage-in-/)

# Production corrections to the current vertical slice

1. Replace the modern stainless-steel twenty-litre keg in 1901 imagery with a small coopered returnable transport cask. Flat brewer's wagons carrying stacked casks are documented for the period. [Karrenmuseum brewer's wagon](https://www.karrenmuseum-databank.be/collectie/829-brouwerswagen.aspx)
2. Change the “fresh yeast crock from the bakehouse” to pitching yeast collected from a neighboring active brewery. The estate granary supplies malt; it is not produced by the bakehouse.
3. Keep **Courtyard Pils** locked behind costly cold-side modernization. Lager was present in Belgium by the late nineteenth century, but it should not behave like an effortless house recipe in this failing 1901 stable brewery.
4. Preserve the current cream/ink/copper, blue-hour “Nocturne in the Old Stables” visual language. In every major shot, foreground a named person's hands doing specific work.
5. Make the 1910 equipment choice visibly additive: new technology is fitted around old limestone and copper rather than replacing the brewery with a modern stainless room.
6. Rename the first winter's **Abbey harvest table** to **Abbey winter table**, or move that contract to the autumn calendar. A harvest feast cannot sit three weeks after a January opening.
